We built the corner shop as infrastructure.

Nairobi already buys groceries in small, frequent baskets. What it did not have was a way to do that without leaving the house or paying a convenience premium. Harraka holds the everyday basket in dark stores across the city and moves it the last two kilometres in under ten minutes.

01 — The company

Started in a Westlands garage, in 2024

The first Harraka store was 40 square metres behind a hardware shop on Chiromo Road, stocked with 180 items and served by three riders on borrowed bikes. We picked that street because we could measure it: every home within two kilometres, and a delivery window we could actually defend.

Two years on the model is the same, only tighter. Three purpose-built dark stores, 2,400 items, 120 salaried riders, and a median delivery under eleven minutes across seven Nairobi zones. We buy for the whole city, so a litre of milk costs less through the app than at the shop on the corner.

We are a Kenyan company, registered in Nairobi, with a team of 214 people. Every rider is on payroll with NHIF and NSSF from their first shift.

02 — What we hold ourselves to

Cheaper than the alternative

If an item costs more through Harraka than at a duka on the same street, that is a pricing bug and we fix it that week.

Ten minutes, or the fee is ours

Past twenty minutes the delivery fee comes off the order automatically. No claim to file, no support queue.

Riders on payroll

A monthly wage, eight-hour shifts, statutory cover, and a bike we own and maintain. Speed is a routing problem, not a rider problem.

Kenyan supply first

Produce comes off contracts with farms in Kiambu and Nakuru, bought weekly at agreed prices rather than on the spot market.
